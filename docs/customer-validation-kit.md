# Customer interviews, task comparisons and paid pilot

Prepared 1 October 2026. No interviews, usability sessions, payments, renewals or retention observations have occurred in this implementation. This is an execution kit. The watchlist is a provisional implementation of the approved hypothesis; interviews can change the priority job before further expansion.

## Initial hypothesis and recruitment

Audience hypothesis: independent equity researchers in India who already compare mutual-fund disclosures while researching stocks. Job hypothesis: understand what changed in followed stocks and verify it against original disclosures. Evidence needed: a recent demonstrated task, repeat frequency, current alternative, actual effort, consequence of errors and willingness to test the next release. Generic interest or compliments do not establish this job.

Recruit 10–15 interviewees, including people outside personal relationships. Record channel and relationship independently from customer role. The owner must authorize contact and select the channel after initial interviews. Two draft channel experiments are direct introductions to independent researchers and permission-based posts in relevant research communities. For each authorized experiment, pre-register audience, invitation/offer, dates and evidence reference; count unique qualified prospects, enrollment, activation, independent payers, cash and founder minutes in one dated cohort. Do not mix cohorts or denominators. These are candidates, not selected or executed channels. Do not scrape/contact member lists.

Unsent invitation draft:

> I am researching how equity researchers compare monthly mutual-fund disclosures. Would you be willing to show a recent research task in a 25-minute conversation? I am looking to understand your current workflow, including tools that already work well. No investment recommendation or purchase is involved. Participation is optional. May I take notes without your name or portfolio contents?

Before recording, separately ask permission. A refusal still permits an interview with minimal written notes if they consent. Explain who can access notes and agree a deletion date. Use participant IDs. Keep contact details and receipt references in a separate private file. Participants need not share account credentials, their full portfolio or sensitive client work.

## Interview guide (25 minutes)

1. Before showing FindIt: “Show the last stock-research task where you looked at mutual-fund holdings. What prompted it? What output did you need?”
2. Observe their existing tool or disclosure workflow. Record reporting month, scope, start/finish, steps, errors and what counted as a correct answer. Ask permission to retain a redacted artifact or reference.
3. “How often did you do this in the last three months? What happens if a fund or month is missing? How do you check a surprising result?”
4. “What do you pay for today? What do those tools do well? What would make this task unnecessary?” Record an actual spend only when supplied, with period/currency. Do not infer budget from occupation.
5. Show one FindIt result and its coverage/evidence. Ask what they think it means before explaining. “Where would this fit in the task you just showed? What would stop you using it?”
6. “Would you test the next actual monthly release on one stock you choose?” Record accepted/declined and their reason. Do not count an interview as activation.

After 10–15 interviews, group demonstrated recurring jobs, alternatives and objections. Separate independent from related participants. Choose one priority job and continue/change the audience explicitly. Several independent demonstrations of the same recurring task and next-release commitments support continuing; absent that pattern, revise the hypothesis. Retain negative and contradictory observations.

## Five unassisted usability sessions

Run after the source review and coverage gaps are understood. Use five new target participants. Read each task once, start a timer and withhold hints. Record completion, seconds, incorrect interpretations, requests for help and the participant's explanation. A moderator intervention means assisted completion. Record desktop/mobile and release identifier.

| Task prompt | Correct interpretation / scoring rubric |
|---|---|
| In August, identify HDFC's largest displayed change and explain its scope. | LIC, +41,618,000 shares in the prepared release; among 29 compared active domestic-equity funds. Recheck the release if data changes. |
| Explain the difference between a fund house and an individual fund. | Several individual funds can contribute to one house; they do not each count as an independent house vote. |
| Identify a house whose displayed result does not represent complete coverage. | SBI: partial 39/40; Kotak/HSBC: unavailable; all-market denominator unknown. Never “the entire market is complete.” |
| Verify a share change from evidence. | Correct month/filter/house, original rows, previous/current/adjusted quantities and opposing changes. Estimated trading value is not execution value or percentage company ownership. |
| Explain a historical gap or apparent reversal. | Fixed cohort and adjacent snapshots; an unavailable full-range cohort is a gap, not zero selling. Buying/selling activity is not proven future performance. |

Targets from the approved plan: at least four of five complete core tasks without help; no participant confuses partial coverage with the entire house; a source-backed result can be reached and explained within one minute. Report each task's actual numerator/denominator and time, not just a combined percentage. Correct confusing parts and rerun affected tasks with new participants. Browser/keyboard checks are engineering evidence, not these observations.

## Competitor task protocol

Current overlap verified from primary pages on 1 October 2026: [RupeeVest](https://www.rupeevest.com/Mutual-Fund-Holdings) describes four-month stock-holding trends, monthly net buying/selling lists, approximate values and downloads. [Trendlyne's own documentation](https://help.trendlyne.com/support/solutions/articles/84000383587-how-can-i-create-a-basket-with-stocks-in-a-mutual-fund-) describes fund holdings, basket analysis and rebalance reminders. These observations do not establish current completeness, accuracy or comparative usability.

Benchmark FindIt, the participant's existing workflow, RupeeVest and Trendlyne on the same stock, reporting month, eligible scope and intended output. Record URL/product version, free/paid access, missing scope and data availability. If a comparable scope is inaccessible, mark “not tested” and explain; do not silently compare unequal datasets or buy access without approval. Counterbalance tool order to reduce familiarity/learning effects.

Tasks: answer what changed; verify one large change; identify incomplete coverage; explain fixed-scope history; save/export the useful output. Record time to a **correct** answer, assistance, source verification, interpretation mistakes and usefulness in the participant's original task. All participant task scores are pending. Select differentiation only after observing an advantage. Auditability, explicit coverage and reproducibility are hypotheses about advantage, not a proven moat.

## Proposed paid-pilot offer (not sent)

“Monthly research report for stocks you follow, with fund-house activity, individual-fund changes, declared coverage, original evidence and consistent-cohort history. Device-only watchlist with import/export. Reports identify their reporting month and data release. No return, alpha or recommendation claim.”

Coverage: the requested eight houses remain the target. Five currently meet complete August comparison scope; SBI is partial; Kotak/HSBC have no obtained portfolios. Do not sell eight-house completeness. The Phase B–E milestone is unfinished. Complete it, or obtain an explicit decision on a narrower disclosed pilot scope, before a paid pilot. History is currently Franklin's 20 active domestic-equity portfolios over February–August, not all houses over seven months.

Frequency: one report per actual new monthly release after source availability and validation, with corrections identified. Do not promise an observed availability date from an assumed regulatory deadline. Delivery is manual/private only if explicitly authorized. Monthly messaging remains conditional on need, recipient consent, unsubscribe handling and duplicate prevention.

Fill two price hypotheses **after** interviews: price, billing period, supporting observed time saving/current spend, included support, cancellation/refund terms and success/failure decision. Both prices remain blank. The owner selects the offer, merchant identity and payment method; actual settled receipts establish payment, not stated willingness or payment-link clicks. Record related, discounted, free, independent, refunded and cancelled participants separately.

Pre-register the approved pilot targets before recruiting: 10 target participants; at least 7 activated; at least 5 meaningfully returning in each of two subsequent real publication cycles; at least 3 independent paying customers; at least 2 renewals. Register release IDs and dates when they really exist. Historical replay does not count as monthly retention.

Activation: one useful watchlist task plus inspecting its evidence. Monthly return: a meaningful research task after a new real data release. Notification opens, staff and test activity are excluded. Use consented structured interviews/event records first. Record only participant ID, release ID, task type, date, outcome and consent; avoid stock lists/search content unless specifically needed and consented. The application currently adds no analytics collector or external transmission.
