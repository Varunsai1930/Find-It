    Reading this page

    Fund houses and individual funds
    A fund house, or asset management company (AMC), runs many individual funds. Individual funds are also called schemes. For example, HDFC Mutual Fund is a fund house, and HDFC Large Cap Fund is one of its funds. Each individual fund has its own portfolio.
    Monthly change
    Each figure compares an individual fund's holdings in one month with its holdings in the previous month. A stock that no fund bought or sold is not ranked. A stock with no monthly change never shows as a buy.
    Net fund houses
    The number of fund houses buying a stock minus the number selling it. For example, 5 buying and 2 selling gives +3, and 2 buying and 5 selling gives −3. The Buying view puts the highest result first. The Selling view puts the lowest result first. The individual-fund counts show how many portfolios changed, not how many fund houses.
    Net buying and discretionary net
    Net buying is the estimated value of purchases minus sales, in ₹ crore, for stocks that the funds already held. A negative amount means net selling. Discretionary net counts only the fund houses whose change is larger than the change that price movement alone would cause. Select More columns to see discretionary net, the value of new positions and FII/DII changes. Each stock's detail also shows them.
    Units
    Flows are in ₹ crore. One crore is 10 million rupees. FII and DII changes are in percentage points (pp) of the company's shares, measured between its two latest quarterly filings.
    Missing, no increase and stale
    missing means the company published no quarterly shareholding filing by the date the month's portfolios became public. FindIt never reads it as zero or as no increase. No increase means two filings exist and the holding did not go up. No prior quarter means only one filing exists, so FindIt cannot measure a change. stale marks a filing that was more than 200 days old on that date.
    FII
    Foreign institutional investors: investment funds and institutions based outside India.
    DII (MF+banks+insurance)
    Domestic institutional investors. FindIt uses a conservative total of mutual funds, banks, insurance companies and other financial institutions.
    What this is not
    This page records what funds did. It is not a list of stocks to buy. It reports changes in share quantity, trading value and holding value. It does not report investment performance.
